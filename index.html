Shadow Triangles

The sun is so far away that its rays arrive parallel. Every object's height, its shadow, and the ray that grazes its top form a right triangle, and every one of those triangles has the same angle at the shadow's tip. Drag the sun, the people, or the handles on top of their heads.

The measurements

Object Height Shadow Height ÷ Shadow
Why aren't the ratios identical? Every measurement gets rounded to the nearest inch, and real measuring adds error:
  • The edge of a shadow is fuzzy (the sun is a disk, not a point), so it's hard to say exactly where it ends.
  • People slouch, stand on tiptoe, or wear shoes.
  • The ground isn't perfectly flat, and the tape may not be perfectly straight.
  • The sun keeps moving while you measure.

All the triangles, stacked

Each triangle is slid over so its shadow tip sits at the same corner. They share that angle θ, so the tops all land on one straight line. Bigger triangles are just scaled-up copies of smaller ones.
