Shadow Triangles

The sun is so far away that its rays arrive parallel. Every object's height, its shadow, and the ray that grazes its top form a right triangle, and every one of those triangles has the same angle at the shadow's tip. Drag the sun, the people, or the handles on top of their heads.

Why aren't the ratios identical? Every measurement gets rounded to the nearest inch, and real measuring adds error:
  • The edge of a shadow is fuzzy (the sun is a disk, not a point), so it's hard to say exactly where it ends.
  • People slouch, stand on tiptoe, or wear shoes.
  • The ground isn't perfectly flat, and the tape may not be perfectly straight.
  • The sun keeps moving while you measure.

All the triangles, stacked

Each triangle is slid over so its shadow tip sits at the same corner. They share that angle θ, so the tops all land on one straight line. Bigger triangles are just scaled-up copies of smaller ones.

Mirror Triangles

Lay a mirror flat on the ground. Light bounces off it at the same angle it arrives, so when you can see the top of something in the mirror, your eye, your feet and the mirror form a small triangle that is similar to the big one. Drag the people, the tree or the mirror until the top lines up, then solve for the height.

Why the triangles are similar
  • Both triangles have a right angle where the person or tree meets the ground.
  • A mirror reflects light at the same angle it arrives: the angle of incidence equals the angle of reflection. Those are measured from the dashed normal line, so the angles with the ground on each side are equal too.
  • Two pairs of equal angles make the triangles similar (AA), so their sides are proportional: H ÷ d₂ = e ÷ d₁.
Doing it outside
  • Lay the mirror flat and mark a dot in its center. Measure all distances to that dot.
  • Use your eye height, not your full height.
  • Step forward or back until the top sits right on the dot, then measure.

Flip one triangle over the mirror

Both triangles are drawn with their mirror corner at the same point, so the student's triangle is flipped over onto the other side. When the top lines up in the mirror, the angles match and the small triangle fits exactly inside the big one.
